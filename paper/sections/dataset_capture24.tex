\section{Dataset and Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:data}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Capture-24 Dataset}\label{sec:data:capture24}

We evaluate on Capture-24~\cite{chan2024capture24}, one of the largest publicly available
free-living datasets of wrist-worn accelerometry with activity annotations. Its free-living
protocol, its size and its natural division into individuals make it a realistic testbed for
federated HAR. Participants are naturally treated as clients, so no synthetic partition is
needed. The data originate from the CAPTURE-24 study (mainly 2014--2015), which compared
time-use diaries with wearable sensors~\cite{gershuny2020testing}. Members of the public in
Oxfordshire, United Kingdom, wore an Axivity AX3 tri-axial accelerometer on the dominant wrist for
about $24$\,h, recording at $100$\,Hz with a dynamic range of $\pm8\,g$. Ground truth during waking
hours came from a wearable camera worn on a lanyard (OMG Life Autographer), which took a
photograph every $20$--$40$\,s, and during the night from a sleep diary. Trained annotators, who had to
reach an inter-rater agreement of Cohen's $\kappa>0.8$ on a gold-standard set, labelled the
recordings with codes from the Compendium of Physical
Activities~\cite{ainsworth2011compendium}. The camera protocol followed an established ethical
framework~\cite{kelly2013ethical}. The images are not released, and for de-identification the ages
are binned and the dates randomized. Of $166$ recruited participants, $151$ remained after quality
control, providing $3{,}883$\,h of recordings of which $2{,}562$\,h are annotated with $206$ distinct
codes. The dataset is distributed under the CC~BY~4.0 licence.

The $206$ codes are mapped to the six-class scheme of Willetts \emph{et
al.}~\cite{willetts2018statistical}, a standard benchmark task on this dataset: \emph{sleep},
\emph{sit/stand}, \emph{mixed activity}, \emph{walking}, \emph{vehicle} and \emph{bicycling}
(\cref{tab:classes}). The task is strongly imbalanced. Sleep and sitting/standing account for
$\PctSleep\,\%$ and $\PctSitStand\,\%$ of the data, whereas bicycling accounts for only
$\PctBicycling\,\%$ (an imbalance ratio of $\ImbalanceRatio{:}1$), and only $\UsersBicycling$ of the
$151$ participants cycle at all. The participants are $99$ women and $52$ men in four age groups
(\cref{tab:partition}).

\subsection{Preprocessing}\label{sec:data:preprocessing}

Each participant's recording is processed independently, as it would be on the device. The
released signals already include the authors' sampling-rate correction and gravity
autocalibration~\cite{chan2024capture24}. We (i)~split the stream into continuous segments wherever
two consecutive timestamps are more than $1.5$ sample periods apart; (ii)~resample each segment from
$100$ to $30$\,Hz with an anti-aliased polyphase filter, the rate at which ssl-wearables and HALO
operate and from which the $20$\,Hz encoders are derived by $\Pi_E$; (iii)~cut non-overlapping $10$\,s
windows ($3\times300$ samples, in $g$); and (iv)~label each window with the majority label of its
$1{,}000$ raw samples, discarding windows whose majority is unannotated. This yields
$\NWindowsFull$ labelled windows ($\NHoursFull$\,h). This total agrees with the $2{,}562$
annotated hours of the release and the $922{,}199$ windows of the official
benchmark~\cite{chan2024capture24}, and the small difference comes from the majority rule. Per
participant, the labelled time ranges from $\HoursPerUserMin$ to $\HoursPerUserMax$\,h (median
$\HoursPerUserMedian$\,h). Windows are stored in half precision with their start time, which the
temporal decoder needs.

To keep the full experimental grid (four encoders, nine training configurations, centralized and
federated) tractable on a single GPU, the experiments use one window per minute, i.e., every sixth
window of each participant. This sub-sampling preserves each participant's diurnal sequence, label
proportions and bout structure at a $1$-min resolution. It yields $\NWindowsExp$ windows, of which the
federated clients hold $\NTrainWindows$ training and $\NTestWindows$ test windows. The pipeline supports
the full resolution without modification.

\subsection{Federated Partition and Data Splits}\label{sec:data:splits}

The split is made in two steps, first by user and then by time (\cref{tab:partition}).

\emph{User level.} The $151$ participants are randomly assigned, with a fixed seed, to three
disjoint groups: \NumClients{} \emph{FL clients} ($70\,\%$), which take part in federated training;
\NumHeldout{} \emph{held-out users} ($20\,\%$), who are never seen during training and measure
generalization to new users; and \NumPool{} \emph{pool users} ($10\,\%$). The pool users provide
\emph{unlabelled} data for the self-supervised pretraining of HALO and serve as validation users for
early stopping and for the choice of $\tau_{\mathrm{inf}}$. No test data are used for any model or
hyperparameter selection. Each client corresponds to exactly one participant, so label skew,
quantity skew and feature shift are all natural.

\emph{Time level.} Within each FL client, the first $80\,\%$ of the chronologically ordered windows
form the training part and the last $20\,\%$ the test part. A random window-level split would place
temporally adjacent and nearly identical windows on both sides and inflate the scores; the
chronological split instead asks the model to recognize the user's \emph{future} activities. For each
held-out user, the first $50\,\%$ is the adaptation part, from which few-shot labels are drawn, and the
last $50\,\%$ the test part on which every new-user protocol is scored.

The centralized references are trained on the union of the clients' training parts and tested on
the same per-client test parts, so centralized and federated results are directly comparable. The
user partition, the device-tier assignment and the client sampling are seeded. The partition files
are released with the code.

\subsection{Statistical Heterogeneity of the Natural Partition}\label{sec:data:hetero}

Federated methods are commonly evaluated on synthetic partitions in which client label
distributions are drawn from a Dirichlet distribution~\cite{hsu2019measuring}. The natural partition
of Capture-24 differs from these partitions in ways that matter for the design of
\cref{sec:method}. \Cref{fig:labelskew} and \cref{tab:hetero} quantify them.

\emph{Skew is concentrated in the rare classes.} All clients observe sit/stand, mixed activity and
walking, and nearly all observe sleep, but only $\ClientsVehiclePct\,\%$ observe vehicle and
$\ClientsBicyclingPct\,\%$ bicycling. A client observes $\ClassesPerClient$ of the six classes on
average, and only $\AllSixPct\,\%$ observe all of them. The mean divergence from the global
distribution ($\JSDMean$ bits) corresponds to a Dirichlet concentration of about $\DirAlpha$, a
moderate skew by the standards of synthetic benchmarks. Its structure, however, is different: the
rare classes are not under-represented but \emph{absent} on most clients. This is the regime in
which a shared classifier trained with cross-entropy loses rare classes
(\cref{sec:method:skew}).

\emph{Temporal shift exceeds client shift.} Because each recording covers roughly one day, a
chronological split separates different parts of the day. On average, sleep accounts for
$\SleepTrainPct\,\%$ of a client's training part but $\SleepTestPct\,\%$ of its test part, and the
test part is mostly sleep for $\TestMostlySleepPct\,\%$ of the clients. The divergence between a
client's own training and test parts (mean $\JSDTrainTest$ bits) is more than three times larger
than the divergence between clients. Decision
rules that copy the local training prior are therefore miscalibrated even for enrolled users,
which motivates calibrating decisions with the population prior (\cref{eq:calibrated}) and modelling
temporal context explicitly. In $\UnseenClientsPct\,\%$ of the clients, the test part contains a
class that the client never observed in training ($\UnseenWindowsPct\,\%$ of all test windows), a case
that only shared knowledge can handle.

\emph{Quantity skew is mild.} The fixed $24$-h protocol makes client sizes similar
($\WinPerClientMin$--$\WinPerClientMax$ training windows, Gini coefficient $\GiniClients$). The
weights $\ptil_k$ are therefore close to uniform, and the heterogeneity is predominantly in the labels and
the users' movement patterns.

\emph{Activities are persistent.} At the $1$-min resolution of the experiments, the diagonal of the
aggregated transition matrix ranges from $\SelfTransMin$ (walking) to $\SelfTransMax$ (sleep), and
the median bout lasts from $2.5$\,min (walking) to more than $3$\,h (sleep; \cref{tab:classes}).
Window-level classifiers ignore this structure; the federated HMM of \cref{sec:method:inference}
exploits it at the cost of $42$ integers per client.

\subsection{Pretrained Encoders}\label{sec:data:encoders}

To test whether the conclusions depend on the backbone, we instantiate \method{} with four IMU
encoders that differ in architecture (Transformer, residual CNN, spatio-temporal graph network,
patch-channel Transformer), in size ($51$\,k to $10.5$\,M frozen parameters), in pretraining corpus
and objective, and in input format (\cref{tab:encoders}). Three are used with their official
checkpoints. The weights are loaded into re-implementations with identical parameter names, and
loading fails if less than $90\,\%$ of the tensors match. HALO~\cite{ding2026halo} has not released
weights. We therefore re-implemented its encoder at a reduced width ($d=192$) and pretrained it with
its stage-1 objectives (masked reconstruction of $50\,\%$ of the $1$-s patches~\cite{he2022masked} and a
patch-level InfoNCE loss~\cite{oord2018representation} at temperature $0.2$) on the $94{,}847$
unlabelled windows of the pool users. Two documented deviations apply: learned channel embeddings
replace the text-derived sensor descriptions, and each token receives the patch mean and log
standard deviation, which per-patch z-scoring would otherwise remove. Because the pool users are
disjoint from the clients and the held-out users, this pretraining leaks no evaluation data.
Capture-24 provides only an accelerometer, so the input adapter feeds zeros to LIMU-BERT's gyroscope
channels at initialization, and UniMTS receives the signal on the right-wrist joint of its
$22$-joint skeleton with zeros elsewhere, as prescribed by its authors~\cite{zhang2024unimts}.

\subsection{Compared Methods}\label{sec:data:methods}

\Cref{tab:methods} lists the federated methods, each defined by the roles of \cref{tab:roles}.
FedAvg trains the whole model from a random initialization. V0 and V1 are the upper-cost and
lower-cost ways of using a pretrained encoder (full fine-tuning and a frozen encoder with
trainable heads). V2 and V3 isolate the aggregation rule for homogeneous LoRA: factor averaging
versus exact aggregation~\cite{singhal2025fedex}. V4 adds rank heterogeneity with the delta-form
aggregation of \cref{sec:method:agg}, and it is the ablation of \method{} (V5) without local parts and
prototypes. All methods with a shared classifier use the label-skew remedies of
\cref{sec:method:skew}, and all methods are scored with the same decoders. Two centralized
references are trained on the pooled training parts: \emph{from scratch}, which defines the target F1,
and \emph{pretrained fine-tuning}, an upper reference without privacy constraints.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}\label{sec:data:metrics}

\emph{Recognition.} The primary metric is the \emph{macro-F1} over the six classes, computed on the
pooled test windows of all clients, so that rare activities weigh as much as frequent ones. We
also report accuracy.

\emph{Fairness.} For every client we compute its macro-F1 over the classes present in its own test
part (a user who never cycles is not scored on cycling). We report the \emph{worst-10\,\% client F1},
i.e., the mean over the $\lceil0.1K\rceil=11$ lowest-scoring clients, and the standard deviation of
the client F1 values.

\emph{Communication.} Every message is an explicit set of tensors. Its size is measured as the
serialized size of uncompressed arrays (four bytes per real value, eight per integer), as in
Flower's NumPy serialization~\cite{beutel2020flower}, and includes every tensor that is sent: factors,
heads, residual weights, prototypes and counts. The one-shot statistics are charged in the first
round. We report the \emph{uplink per client update} (MB), the \emph{rounds to target}, i.e., the first
evaluated round whose macro-F1 reaches $90\,\%$ of the centralized from-scratch macro-F1 of the same
encoder (evaluations every $8$ rounds), and the cumulative uplink of all clients up to that round
(\emph{MB to target}). No compression or quantization is applied, since it would benefit all methods
equally.

\emph{Client cost.} The \emph{client memory} is the size of all model tensors plus the peak additional
device memory allocated during a local update (activations, gradients and optimizer states), measured
with the CUDA allocator. The \emph{client time} is the wall-clock time of one local update.

\emph{New users.} On the \NumHeldout{} held-out users we report the mean per-user macro-F1 on their test
halves: \emph{zero-shot} (no labels) and \emph{few-shot} with $m\in\{1,5\}$ minutes of labels per
activity, drawn at random from the adaptation half. For methods with a shared classifier, the
few-shot protocol re-fits the classifier on the frozen embeddings.

\emph{Decoding.} Every metric is reported per window and after federated HMM decoding (``+HMM'').
For methods with a shared classifier, we also report the common ``FedAvg + local fine-tuning''
protocol of personalized FL (``+FT''): every client fine-tunes only the classifier on its own
training part ($8$ steps, the same loss as in training) and is scored with this personal classifier.
\method{} needs no such step, because its classifier is already personal.

\subsection{Implementation Details}\label{sec:data:impl}

All methods run in one transparent federated simulator written in PyTorch~2.11 (Python~3.13). All
windows reside in GPU memory, so a mini-batch is an index tensor and every method sees exactly the same
data stream for a given seed. Experiments ran on a single NVIDIA GeForce GTX~1660~Ti (6\,GB) in fp32.
\Cref{tab:hparams} lists the hyperparameters. They are identical for all encoders except UniMTS. Its
graph network costs about $7$\,GMAC per window, so it trains with a reduced budget ($40$ rounds,
batch size $16$, $20$ few-shot steps, intermediate evaluations on $60$ test windows per client and at most
$200$ test windows per held-out user). UniMTS results are therefore comparable across methods, but
not with the other encoders. All local updates use AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with a fresh
optimizer state in every round.
